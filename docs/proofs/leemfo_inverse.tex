\title{The fastest correct inverse for strong-phase LEEM:\\
discrete Jacobi--Anger fitting, not pixel Gauss--Newton}
\author{Wilson Lau}
\date{24 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Linearized CTF inversion is biased for the kinematic LEEM phase object
$\psi=\exp(\ii\varphi\sin(kx))$ once $\varphi=4\pi A/\lambda_0$ is no longer
small. Among four candidate inverses---Gauss--Newton on bilinear Fourier
optics (FO), Jacobi--Anger discrete-mode fitting, Gerchberg--Saxton with an
FO projector, and phase-diversity / MAL---only the first two are
well-posed for the Yu--Lau--Altman forward model, and only the second is
cheap. For 1D periodic ripples the correct unknown is a handful of Bessel
coefficients inside the contrast aperture, not the $\Nap$ aperture pixels.
That reduces a bilinear solve of cost $O(\Nap^2)$ to an $O(M^2)$ evaluation
with $M=2\lfloor\qap\Lambda\rfloor+1\ll\Nap$. For general 2D topography the
same reduction is unavailable; the fastest still-correct method is then
damped Gauss--Newton on bilinear FO, initialized by the linearized CTF.
Gerchberg--Saxton is not a distinct correct algorithm under partial
coherence, and MAL / phase diversity is the same Gauss--Newton residual
stacked over extra defoci (needed only when aberrations are unknown or
CTF zeros must be filled). We recommend a two-stage pipeline and a
precise skip test for stage~2, and we identify the Lean theorems that
make this inverse formalizable.
\end{abstract}

\section{The inverse that the forward model actually poses}

Image intensity in the Yu--Lau--Altman FO model is the bilinear form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:I}
I(\mathbf{r})
=\iint
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\mathbf{q})\,\boldsymbol{\Psi}^*(\mathbf{q}')
\,\RFO(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}',\Dz)
\,\ee^{2\pi\ii(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}')\cdot\mathbf{r}}
\,\dd^2q\,\dd^2q',
\end{equation}
with $\RFO$ supported on the contrast-aperture disk
$\abs{\mathbf{q}},\abs{\mathbf{q}'}\le\qap$~\cite{Yu2019}.
A separable CTF kernel $\RCTF$ agrees with $\RFO$ on the axes
$\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0}$ or $\mathbf{q}'=\mathbf{0}$
(already a Lean theorem: \texttt{R\_FO\_eq\_R\_CTF\_axis}),
and $\RCTF=\RFO\,\Gamma_C\Gamma_S$ off axis.
Linearized (weak-phase) inversion uses only those axes: if
$\psi=\sigma(1+\ii\phi)+O(\phi^2)$, the contrast at frequency $\mathbf{q}$
is proportional to $\RFO(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{0})$, where $\Gamma_C\Gamma_S=1$.
The linear inverse is therefore the correct first-order Fr\'echet derivative
of~\eqref{eq:I} even under partial coherence.

It is biased as soon as quadratic products
$\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\mathbf{q})\boldsymbol{\Psi}^*(\mathbf{q}')$ with
\emph{both} $\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}'\neq\mathbf{0}$ rise above noise.
For a sinusoidal corrugation those products are not a perturbation: they
are the image.

\section{Object classes, $\Nap$ and $M$}

\begin{definition}[Aperture degrees of freedom]
Let $L$ be the field of view and $\Delta q=1/L$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Nap}
\Nap
:=
\#\{\text{sampled }\mathbf{q}:\abs{\mathbf{q}}\le\qap\}
=
\begin{cases}
2\lfloor \qap L\rfloor+1, & \text{1D lineout},\\[0.3em]
\pi(\qap L)^2, & \text{2D circular aperture}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
On a Nyquist grid $N=2\qap L$ one has $\Nap=\pi N^2/4$ in 2D
and $\Nap=N$ in 1D.
\end{definition}

IBM-style parameters~\cite{Schramm2012,Yu2019}
($E_0=10\,\mathrm{eV}$, $\lambda_0\simeq 0.388\,\mathrm{nm}$,
column $\lambda\simeq 0.010\,\mathrm{nm}$, information limit
$\sim 1$--$2\,\mathrm{nm}$) give $\qap\sim 0.5$--$1\,\mathrm{nm}^{-1}$.
A $2\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ field of view then has
$\Nap^{\mathrm{1D}}\sim 2\times 10^3$ and
$\Nap^{\mathrm{2D}}\sim 3\times 10^6$
(or $\Nap^{\mathrm{2D}}\sim 5\times 10^4$ on a $256^2$ Nyquist patch).
Naive assembly of~\eqref{eq:I} is $O(\Nap^2)$: this is why FO is
slow compared with a CTF FFT of cost $O(N\log N)$~\cite{Yu2017,Yu2019}.

\begin{definition}[Bessel modes of a 1D ripple]
\label{def:M}
For $\psi(x)=\exp(\ii\varphi\sin(2\pi x/\Lambda))$ the Jacobi--Anger
theorem (Lean: \texttt{jacobi\_anger}) gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:JA}
\psi(x)=\sum_{n\in\Z}J_n(\varphi)\,\ee^{2\pi\ii n x/\Lambda},
\qquad
q_n=n/\Lambda.
\end{equation}
The aperture keeps only
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:M}
M
:=2n_{\mathrm{ap}}+1,
\qquad
n_{\mathrm{ap}}=\bigl\lfloor\min(\qap\Lambda,\,n_\ast(\varphi))\bigr\rfloor,
\end{equation}
where $n_\ast(\varphi)\simeq\abs{\varphi}+2\abs{\varphi}^{1/3}$ is the
Olver turning point of $J_n(\varphi)$ (practically $\abs{\varphi}+3$).
Bessel decay, not the pixel grid, sets $M$ once $\qap\Lambda>\abs{\varphi}$.
\end{definition}

Kinematic amplitude $\varphi=4\pi A/\lambda_0$ is large for ordinary
LEEM topography: $A=0.2\,\mathrm{nm}$ already gives $\varphi\simeq 6.5$;
a $1\,\mathrm{nm}$ wrinkle gives $\varphi\simeq 32$.
Typical counts:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
Object & $\Lambda$ & $\varphi$ & $M$ & $\Nap$ (1D, $L=2\,\mu\mathrm{m}$) \\
\midrule
thermal graphene ripple & $10\,\mathrm{nm}$ & $\sim 2$ & $\sim 11$ & $\sim 2\times 10^3$ \\
LEEM wrinkle / Yu test sinusoid & $100$--$500\,\mathrm{nm}$ & $10$--$10^2$ & $10^2$--$10^3$ & $\sim 2\times 10^3$ \\
general 1D topography & --- & --- & $\Nap$ & $\sim 2\times 10^3$ \\
general 2D topography, $256^2$ & --- & --- & $\Nap$ & $\Nap\sim 5\times 10^4$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The Yu sinusoidal object therefore lives in a subspace of dimension
$M\sim 10$--$10^3$, not $\Nap$ and not the pixel count $N$.

\section{Four candidates}

\subsection*{A. Nonlinear Gauss--Newton on bilinear FO}

Unknown: the aperture spectrum $\boldsymbol{\Psi}$ (or a real phase $\phi(\mathbf{r})$
of length $\Nap$). Residual $I_{\mathrm{FO}}(\boldsymbol{\Psi})-I_{\mathrm{obs}}$.
Each iteration evaluates~\eqref{eq:I} in $O(\Nap^2)$ (fill the
difference-frequency histogram, then one FFT) and applies the adjoint
of the bilinear map. A damped Gauss--Newton / Levenberg--Marquardt step
with inner CG costs
\begin{equation}
O\bigl(k\,n_{\mathrm{CG}}\,\Nap^2\bigr)
\qquad
(k\sim 5\text{--}20\text{ outer iterations if the linear init is in basin}).
\end{equation}
Initialized by linearized CTF this is \emph{locally} the correct
maximum-likelihood estimator for Gaussian noise and known optics.
It is not globally unique from a single defocus (CTF zeros; bilinear
phase retrieval). For a sinusoid it is correct but spends $\Nap^2/M^2$
useless work on empty Fourier bins.

\subsection*{B. Jacobi--Anger discrete-mode fitting}

Substitute~\eqref{eq:JA} into~\eqref{eq:I} and drop modes with
$\abs{n}>\qap\Lambda$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:I-modes}
I(x)
=\sum_{n,m=-n_{\mathrm{ap}}}^{n_{\mathrm{ap}}}
J_n(\varphi)\,J_m(\varphi)\,
\RFO(q_n,q_m,\Dz)\,
\ee^{2\pi\ii(q_n-q_m)x}.
\end{equation}
If the object is known to be a single sinusoid, the unknown is
$(\varphi,x_0,\sigma)$ --- three real numbers --- or even $\varphi$
alone once the period $\Lambda$ is read from the fundamental peak.
Each residual costs $O(M^2)$, independently of $N$ after the
$2n_{\mathrm{ap}}$ measured harmonics of $I$ have been extracted
($O(N\log N)$ once).

Unconstrained $\{c_n\}$ with $c_n=J_n(\varphi)$ \emph{not} imposed
is a bilinear phase-retrieval problem of size $M$ and is the wrong
encoding: the physics is a one-parameter Bessel curve, not $M$ free
complex amplitudes. Least squares \emph{on} $J_n(\varphi)$ means the
parametric map $\varphi\mapsto (J_n(\varphi))_n$.

Sign of $\varphi$: $J_n(-\varphi)=(-1)^n J_n(\varphi)$, so odd
intensity harmonics flip with $\mathrm{sign}(\varphi)$. Convex versus
concave ripples are identifiable. Local uniqueness fails only at
isolated Bessel extrema, which a 1D line search on $\varphi$ skips.

This is exact for the object class that makes linearized CTF fail in
Yu~\emph{et al.}~\cite{Yu2017,Yu2019}, and it is already half-formalized
(\texttt{jacobi\_anger}, \texttt{besselJ\_bound}).

\subsection*{C. Gerchberg--Saxton with an FO projector}

Classical GS replaces moduli in two planes linked by a unitary FFT.
Under partial coherence, $I$ is \emph{not} $\abs{\psi_{\mathrm{img}}}^2$
of a single coherent wave: the correct intensity constraint is the
quadratic set $\{\psi:\text{\eqref{eq:I}}=I_{\mathrm{obs}}\}$.
The metric projector onto that set \emph{is} a Gauss--Newton step on
the FO residual. So a correctly specified ``GS+FO projector'' is A
with worse globalization (stagnation, no damping, no Gauss--Newton
Hessian). A na\"ive modulus-replacement projector is the coherent CTF
model and is biased precisely when $\Gamma_C\Gamma_S\neq 1$ off axis.
GS is therefore either incorrect or a slower, less stable rewrite of A.
It is a poor Lean target: no uniqueness theorem, no closed residual.

\subsection*{D. Phase diversity / MAL}

Phase diversity~\cite{Paxman1992} jointly estimates object and
aberrations from $\Nf$ known-diversity images.
MAL~\cite{Coene1996} (Coene--Thust maximum-likelihood, typically
initialized by the paraboloid method PAM) is Gauss--Newton on the
\emph{stacked} FO residual of a focal series.
Cost
\begin{equation}
O\bigl(\Nf\,k\,n_{\mathrm{CG}}\,\Nap^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Statistically efficient, fills CTF zeros, and is the right estimator
when $\chi$ is unknown. When optics are known and the object is a
1D sinusoid, extra defoci are unused degrees of freedom: one
non-zero CTF sample at $q=1/\Lambda$ already identifies $\varphi$
in~\eqref{eq:I-modes}. D is A with more data, not a faster inverse.

\noindent\textit{Pick for the Lean/PDF formalization of Yu's sinusoid:}
\textbf{B}, with linearized CTF as the cheap initializer (stage~1)
and with A recorded as the 2D generalisation.

\section{Two-stage method}

\paragraph{Stage 1 (always).}
Linear CTF / Wiener inversion on the $q'=0$ slice, cost $O(N\log N)$
or $O(n_{\mathrm{ap}})$ after extracting harmonics.
For a sinusoid: read $\Lambda$ from the fundamental of $I$, divide the
complex amplitude at $q=1/\Lambda$ by $\RFO(q,0,\Dz)$
(equals $\RCTF(q,0,\Dz)$), obtain an apparent amplitude $\varphi_{\mathrm{lin}}$.
This $\varphi_{\mathrm{lin}}$ tracks $2J_1(\varphi)$ rather than $\varphi$
(standard weak-phase bias). Compute the FO residual
$r_1=\norm{I_{\mathrm{FO}}(\varphi_{\mathrm{lin}})-I_{\mathrm{obs}}}$.

\paragraph{Stage 2 (1D periodic).}
Scalar (or 3-parameter) Gauss--Newton / golden-section search on
\eqref{eq:I-modes}:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\varphi,x_0}
\sum_{x}
\Bigl\lvert
I_{\mathrm{obs}}(x)
-I_{\mathrm{FO}}\bigl(\{J_n(\varphi)\}\bigr)
\Bigr\rvert^2.
\end{equation}
Jacobian in $\varphi$ is analytic:
$J_n'(\varphi)=\tfrac12(J_{n-1}(\varphi)-J_{n+1}(\varphi))$.
Cost $O(k M^2)$ with $k\sim 5$--$20$. This is A \emph{restricted to the
Jacobi--Anger manifold}, which is why it stays correct and becomes cheap.

\paragraph{Stage 2 (2D general).}
A few damped Gauss--Newton iterations on~\eqref{eq:I} in the pixel
(or aperture) basis, initialized by stage~1. Optional: CTF Jacobian
with FO residual (inexact GN). Use D / a focal series only if
stage~1 hits a CTF zero or $\chi$ is part of the unknown.

\paragraph{When stage 2 is unnecessary.}
The linear term of~\eqref{eq:I} lives on the axes, where
$\RFO=\RCTF$. Quadratic (and higher) Bessel products are the whole
bias. Skip stage~2 when they sit below noise:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.35em]
\item \textbf{Weak phase.}
$\max\abs{\varphi}\lesssim 0.3$
(i.e.\ $A\lesssim 0.01\,\mathrm{nm}$ at $E_0=10\,\mathrm{eV}$).
Then $J_1(\varphi)=\varphi/2+O(\varphi^3)$ and
$\abs{J_2/J_1}\simeq\abs{\varphi}/4\lesssim 0.08$.
\item \textbf{Aperture already kills higher orders, and $\varphi$ is still
moderate.}
If $\qap\Lambda<2$ then $M=3$ ($n=0,\pm 1$). The remaining nonlinearity
is $J_0(\varphi)\approx 1-\varphi^2/4$ and $J_1(\varphi)\approx\varphi/2$.
Skip only if that $\varphi^2$ error is below noise, not merely because
$M=3$.
\item \textbf{$\chi^2$ test on the FO residual of stage~1.}
If $r_1^2/\sigma^2$ is consistent with the noise model, the quadratic
terms are undetectable; stage~2 cannot improve the estimate.
This is the operational skip, and it covers ``$\Gamma_C\Gamma_S\approx 1$
on populated pairs'' automatically: those pairs only enter at quadratic
order.
\end{enumerate}

Do \emph{not} skip stage~2 merely because $\Gamma_C\Gamma_S\approx 1$.
On the axes $\Gamma=1$ always; the linear inverse never saw the
off-axis envelopes. Strong-phase bias is a property of $J_n(\varphi)$,
not of partial coherence. Partial coherence changes the \emph{weights}
$\RFO(q_n,q_m)$ in~\eqref{eq:I-modes} and so must sit in the
\emph{forward} model of stage~2, but it does not by itself decide
whether stage~2 is needed.

\section{What to encode in Lean (and what not)}

Formalize the \emph{forward identity} of B and the linearization
bias; do not formalize GS iterates or a MAL driver.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.35em]
\item Discrete spectrum of $\mathrm{e}^{\ii\varphi\sin(kx)}$
(corollary of \texttt{jacobi\_anger}): support on $q_n=n/\Lambda$.
\item Aperture section: $M=2\lfloor\qap\Lambda\rfloor+1$ modes;
Bessel tail $\sum_{\abs{n}>N}\abs{J_n(\varphi)}\le 2(\abs{\varphi}+\varphi^2)/N$
from \texttt{besselJ\_bound}.
\item Finite bilinear image~\eqref{eq:I-modes} as a double sum of
length $M^2$ (complexity theorem: \texttt{card\_modePairs}).
\item Linearization: the Fr\'echet derivative of~\eqref{eq:I} at
$\varphi=0$ is the axis operator $\RCTF=\RFO(\,\cdot\,,0)$
(reuse \texttt{R\_FO\_eq\_R\_CTF\_axis}).
\item Stage-2 skip: if $\sum_{\abs{n}\ge 2}\abs{J_n(\varphi)}^2<\varepsilon$
then the linear inverse's relative error is $O(\varepsilon+\varphi^2)$.
\end{enumerate}

Items C and D are algorithms, not identities. A in 2D is local
convergence of Gauss--Newton on a bilinear map --- true, but it does
not use any LEEM-specific theorem beyond~\eqref{eq:I}. The
LEEM-specific, already half-written content is B.

\section{Conclusion}

For the object that the FO/CTF papers actually use to exhibit
linearization failure --- 1D sinusoidal / strong-phase ripples ---
the fastest still-correct inverse is Jacobi--Anger discrete-mode
fitting (B), not pixel Gauss--Newton, not GS, not MAL.
Cost $O(M^2)$ with $M=2\lfloor\min(\qap\Lambda,\abs{\varphi}+3)\rfloor+1$,
versus $O(\Nap^2)$ for A and $O(\Nf\Nap^2)$ for D.
For 2D general objects B does not apply; use linearized CTF
initialization plus a few Gauss--Newton steps on bilinear FO (A),
and invoke phase diversity / MAL only when the optics are unknown
or a single defocus is CTF-blind.
Stage~2 of the two-stage pipeline is unnecessary when the FO residual
of the linear reconstruction is at the noise floor, equivalently when
$\max\abs{\varphi}\lesssim 0.3$ for typical LEEM SNR.
