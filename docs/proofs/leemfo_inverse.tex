\title{Through-focal inversion of LEEM intensities\\
with the Fourier-optics kernel}
\author{Wilson Lau}
\date{25 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present a through-focal inversion of low-energy electron microscopy
(LEEM) intensities for the object exit wave
$\psi_0=\sigma\,\ee^{\ii\phi}$, based on the Fourier-optics (FO) kernel
of Yu, Lau and Altman~\cite{Yu2019}.
Prior FO studies compared predicted and measured contrast for prescribed
objects; they did not recover $\psi_0$.
We invert that map in two stages: regularized multi-defocus inversion
on the contrast-transfer slice $\RFO(\mathbf{q},0,\Dz)$, which fills
zeros of a single-defocus contrast-transfer function (CTF), followed
by optional Gauss--Newton iteration on the full bilinear kernel,
omitted when the linearized residual lies at the noise floor
($\max\abs{\phi}\lesssim 0.3$).
A one-dimensional sinusoid reduces the second stage to Jacobi--Anger
fitting of the aperture-truncated Bessel ladder.
Uniqueness of the regularized Fourier-diagonal estimator and its
bias--noise identity are machine-checked in Lean~4 against that kernel.
This work extends quantitative evaluation of LEEM contrast from forward
matching of a focal series to recovery of the object that produced it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Image interpretation in LEEM~\cite{Bauer1994,Bauer2014,Tromp2000,Altman2010}
requires a quantitative account of how the cathode lens, contrast
aperture, source size and energy spread map the object exit wave to
the recorded intensity.
Aberration-corrected instruments~\cite{Tromp2010,Tromp2013} make that
map sharper, but they do not remove the distinction between two
standard calculations.
The contrast-transfer function (CTF) evaluates the inverse transforms
of $\boldsymbol{\Psi}H$ and $\boldsymbol{\Psi}^*H^*$ separately and is computationally
efficient~\cite{Schramm2012,Tromp2012}.
Fourier optics (FO) retains inseparable mixing of spatial-frequency
pairs $(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}')$ under partial
coherence~\cite{Pang2009,Yu2017,Yu2019}.
The mathematically rigorous FO calculation gains an advantage under
conditions, particularly defocus, in which partial coherence
compromises the validity of the CTF intensity~\cite{Yu2019}.
Yu, Locatelli and Altman~\cite{Yu2017} showed that this distinction is
not academic: CTF modelling fails for strongly rippled graphene,
whereas FO reproduces the measured through-focal contrast.

Those studies prescribe an object, evaluate the forward map, and
compare predicted and measured contrast versus defocus.
They do not invert.
The only demonstrated 2D experimental LEEM exit-wave reconstruction is
the linear focal-series restoration of Duden~\emph{et
al.}~\cite{Duden2014}, in the tradition of
Schiske~\cite{Schiske1968,Schiske1973}, assuming a strong specular beam
and a CTF-style model.
Here the same FO forward map is inverted: given a through-focal
intensity series $I(\mathbf{r},\Dz_k)$, $k=1,\dots,K$, we recover
$\psi_0$ within the aperture cutoff, up to a global phase.
The Lean identities of the companion note~\cite{Lau2026proofs} are the
prerequisite: the discrete inverse is checked against that kernel,
not against a substitute propagator.

A single defocus underdetermines a general complex object.
The CTF factor may vanish on interior rings; the contrast aperture
discards $\abs{\mathbf{q}}>\qap$; intensity is invariant under a global
phase; and one complex measurement cannot identify the conjugate pair
$\bigl(X(\mathbf{q}),X(-\mathbf{q})\bigr)$.
A focal series with non-coincident zeros restores those modes that the
aperture admits.
We compare five inverses and recommend a two-stage estimator that
uses the Yu~2019 kernel on the CTF slice
$\RFO(\mathbf{q},0,\Dz)$ and, when required, the full bilinear
residual.
Selected algebraic properties of the discrete linear stage are
machine-checked in Lean~4~\cite{deMoura2021} against that kernel
(\cref{sec:lean,app:formal}).

\section{Image formation}
\label{sec:formation}

The object is the complex exit wave
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:psi0}
\psi_0(\mathbf{r})=\sigma(\mathbf{r})\,\ee^{\ii\phi(\mathbf{r})},
\end{equation}
with $\phi$ in radians.
For a topographic height $A$ in reflection the kinematic phase is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phiA}
\phi=\frac{4\pi A}{\lambda_0},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_0$ is the wavelength at the sample.
At $E_0=10\,\mathrm{eV}$, $\lambda_0\simeq 0.39\,\mathrm{nm}$, so
$A=0.1\,\mathrm{nm}$ already gives $\phi\sim 3$, outside the
weak-phase regime occupied by graphene ripples and atomic
steps~\cite{Yu2017}.
Lens optics use the wavelength $\lambda$ after acceleration to column
energy $E$ (typically $15\,\mathrm{kV}$): spatial frequency is
$\mathbf{q}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}/\lambda$~\cite{Tromp2012,Schramm2012}.
The reconstruction kernel below is written in $\lambda$; conversion of
reconstructed phase to height uses $\lambda_0$.

With $\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\mathcal{F}[\psi_0]$, the FO intensity is bilinear
in the object spectrum~\cite{Goodman2005,Pang2009,Yu2019},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:I}
I(\mathbf{r},\Dz)
=\iint
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\mathbf{q})\,\boldsymbol{\Psi}^*(\mathbf{q}')
\,\RFO(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}',\Dz)
\,\ee^{2\pi\ii(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}')\cdot\mathbf{r}}
\,\dd^2q\,\dd^2q'.
\end{equation}
The kernel is supported on the contrast aperture
$\abs{\mathbf{q}},\abs{\mathbf{q}'}\le\qap$ and factors as
$\RFO=R_0 E_S E_{C,\mathrm{tot}}$, with closed-form spatial and
chromatic envelopes derived in~\cite{Yu2019} and proved
in~\cite{Lau2026proofs}.
The CTF kernel agrees with $\RFO$ on the slice
$\mathbf{q}'=\mathbf{0}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:slice}
\RFO(\mathbf{q},0,\Dz)=\RCTF(\mathbf{q},\Dz).
\end{equation}
The axis $\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0}$ is the conjugate branch
$\RFO(0,-\mathbf{q})$, not the CTF.
Wave aberration $\chi$ is measured in waves, with transfer factor
$W=\ee^{2\pi\ii\chi}$~\cite{Tromp2012}.
Defocus enters $\chi_S$ as $+\tfrac12\Dz\lambda q^2$ in the convention
of this work; mixing the opposite sign used in some simulators
interchanges overfocus and underfocus.

Intensity is invariant under $\psi_0\mapsto\ee^{\ii\theta}\psi_0$.
Modes with $\abs{\mathbf{q}}>\qap$ are identically invisible in every
defocus.
The linearization of~\eqref{eq:I} about a strong specular beam
therefore recovers $\psi_0$ only up to a global phase (fixed here by
taking the zero-frequency component real and positive) and only inside
the aperture.

\section{Through-focal inversion}
\label{sec:inverse}

Let $N$ denote the number of image pixels and $K$ the number of
defoci; a typical stack is $N=512^2$ and $K\sim 11$--$21$.
Regularization of an ill-posed filter follows
Tikhonov~\cite{Tikhonov1963} and Wiener~\cite{Wiener1949}: a positive
floor $\alpha$ in the denominator prevents division by a vanished CTF
at the price of a bias of order $\alpha$.

\subsection{Candidate methods}
\label{sec:five}

Single-image Wiener deconvolution applies one Fourier transform and
divides by $\RCTF(\mathbf{q},\Dz)$.
Interior CTF zeros leave undetermined modes, and one plane does not
identify a general complex object.

Transport of intensity~\cite{Teague1983,Paganin1998} recovers phase
from two or three closely spaced defoci under a paraxial continuity
equation.
After acceleration, LEEM has $\lambda\sim 10\,\mathrm{pm}$, a hard
aperture, spherical aberration and partial coherence.
That PDE is not an inverse of~\eqref{eq:I} and has not been the
method used on experimental LEEM graphene or steps.

Multi-defocus Schiske restoration~\cite{Schiske1968,Duden2014}
transforms each plane and treats spatial frequencies independently.
The slice multipliers are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linear}
h_k(\mathbf{q})=\RFO(\mathbf{q},0,\Dz_k).
\end{equation}
For a single unknown amplitude $X(\mathbf{q})$ the regularized
estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:xhat}
\hat x
=
\frac{\sum_k \overline{h_k}\,y_k}{\alpha+\sum_k\abs{h_k}^2}.
\end{equation}
Linearization about vacuum couples $X(\mathbf{q})$ with
$X(-\mathbf{q})$; the corresponding $2\times 2$ regularized system
uses the same $\alpha$ and is not~\eqref{eq:xhat}.
If one defocus has $h_k=0$, the remaining planes can still determine
the bin.
The cost is $K{+}1$ discrete Fourier transforms plus $O(KN)$ arithmetic.
This is Duden's algorithm with the Yu~2019 envelopes, and it coincides
with one Gauss--Newton step from vacuum on~\eqref{eq:I}.

Gerchberg--Saxton iteration~\cite{GerchbergSaxton1972} and hybrid
input--output~\cite{Fienup1982} replace the computed modulus by the
measured intensity and propagate with a coherent lens model.
Under partial coherence the intensity constraint is the quadratic set
of~\eqref{eq:I}.
Naive modulus replacement is therefore the coherent CTF model and is
biased when FO and CTF disagree off axis~\cite{Yu2017}.
Projection onto the FO intensity is a slower, undamped rewrite of
nonlinear least squares.

Nonlinear least squares minimizes the bilinear FO residual of the
stack.
Each Gauss--Newton step uses the pair kernel, so it sees the mixing
dropped by~\eqref{eq:xhat}.
That estimator is FO-faithful for $\phi\not\ll 1$, at the cost of
pair accumulations rather than a Fourier-diagonal solve.
If the aberrations themselves are unknown, the same idea stacked over
extra defoci is the maximum-likelihood (MAL) reconstruction of Coene,
Thust, Op de Beeck and Van Dyck~\cite{Coene1996}.

\subsection{Recommended estimator}
\label{sec:verdict}

For a general 2D experimental stack the fastest method that still
fills CTF zeros is multi-defocus regularized inversion on
the slice~\eqref{eq:slice}.
It reads all $KN$ Fourier samples, fills interior zeros of
$\sin(2\pi\chi_S)$ by stacking defoci, and has a unique minimizer for
every $\alpha>0$ (\cref{thm:unique}).

The FO-faithful estimator initializes that reconstruction and applies
a few damped Gauss--Newton steps on the bilinear residual (MAL if
aberrations are unknown).
The nonlinear stage is omitted when the FO residual of the linear
reconstruction already lies at the noise floor, equivalently
$\max\abs{\phi}\lesssim 0.3$ at typical LEEM signal-to-noise
($A\lesssim 0.01\,\mathrm{nm}$ at $E_0=10\,\mathrm{eV}$).
The linearization never sees off-axis pairs
$(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}')$ at which FO and CTF disagree; that omitted
term is the quadratic remainder of~\eqref{eq:I} about vacuum
(\cref{thm:quad}) and is the whole image for a strong-phase
sinusoid~\cite{Yu2017}.
Agreement of the slice~\eqref{eq:slice} with a CTF calculation is
therefore not a sufficient skip criterion.

For the one-dimensional sinusoid of period $\Lambda$ used
in~\cite{Yu2017,Yu2019} to exhibit CTF failure, the unknown reduces to
an amplitude $\varphi$ and a shift.
The aperture retains
$M=2\lfloor\qap\Lambda\rfloor+1$ modes.
The FO image is then a double sum of $M^2$ Jacobi--Anger terms,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:JA}
\ee^{\ii\varphi\sin(2\pi x/\Lambda)}
=\sum_{n\in\Z}J_n(\varphi)\,\ee^{2\pi\ii n x/\Lambda},
\end{equation}
and least squares on $\varphi$ with
$J_n'=\tfrac12(J_{n-1}-J_{n+1})$ is $O(M^2)$.
A general 2D micrograph has no Bessel ladder; the nonlinear stage
remains Gauss--Newton in the aperture basis.

\subsection{Discrete procedure}
\label{sec:algo}

The linear stage is the unique minimizer of \cref{thm:unique}.
After rigid alignment, each plane is mean-subtracted and
discrete-Fourier transformed; the zero-frequency bin, which carries
total flux ($\RFO(0,0)=1$), is treated separately.
For every $\abs{\mathbf{q}}\le\qap$ and every recorded defocus the
slice~\eqref{eq:linear} is evaluated from the Yu~2019 envelopes.
The scalar estimator~\eqref{eq:xhat} or the conjugate-pair
$2\times 2$ system is solved at that bin with fixed $\alpha>0$;
coefficients with $\abs{\mathbf{q}}>\qap$ are set to zero.
An inverse transform yields $\sigma=\abs{\hat\psi}$ and
$\phi=\arg(\hat\psi)$ in the vacuum gauge.
Experimental lens current is mapped onto the defocus convention of
\cref{sec:formation} by one fitted affine transform.

\section{Machine-checked properties of the discrete estimator}
\label{sec:lean}

Selected algebraic claims for the discrete linear estimator are
machine-checked in Lean~4~\cite{deMoura2021,mathlib2020} against the
FO kernel encoded from Yu~\emph{et al.}~\cite{Yu2019,Lau2026proofs}.
The statements are finite-dimensional (finite defoci and a finite
frequency grid).
The discrepancy $n_k$ that enters the error identity is deterministic
(linearization remainder, instrument noise and discretization); no
probability space is introduced.
Full statements are collected in \cref{app:formal}.

The reconstruction uses~\eqref{eq:slice}, not a substitute
propagator.
For $\alpha>0$ the regularized energy at each frequency bin has a
unique global minimizer~\eqref{eq:xhat} (\cref{thm:unique}).
If $y_k=h_k x^\star+n_k$, the estimation error is exactly
$(\sum_k\overline{h_k}\,n_k-\alpha x^\star)/D$ with
$D=\alpha+\sum_k\abs{h_k}^2$ (\cref{thm:error}); a sharp triangle
bound follows (\cref{thm:bound}).
Frequencies outside the aperture reconstruct as zero at every
defocus; a global phase is invisible; one measurement does not
identify $\bigl(X(\mathbf{q}),X(-\mathbf{q})\bigr)$; and
$\sin(2\pi\chi_S)$ vanishes on interior rings of a pure-defocus
column ($C_3=C_5=0$, $\Dz\neq 0$, large enough aperture), which is
why $K\ge 2$ is required (\cref{thm:ctfzero}).
The multi-defocus slice map is the derivative of bilinear FO at
vacuum; the neglected term is homogeneous of degree two
(\cref{thm:quad}).
When that remainder lies above the noise, the nonlinear stage is
the consistent next estimator; the numerical skip criterion
$\max\abs{\phi}\lesssim 0.3$ is not part of the machine-checked
fragment.

A modelled operation count, treating a discrete Fourier transform as
$N\log_2 N$ operations, shows that the linear stage is cheaper than a
dense pair apply already at $N=128$.
Existence of a fast Fourier transform, wall-clock times, Gaussian or
Poisson statistics, Cram\'er--Rao bounds, Gauss--Newton convergence,
image alignment and the current-to-defocus map are not claimed.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:end}

We invert the FO image-formation map of Yu, Lau and
Altman~\cite{Yu2019} for a 2D through-focal intensity series.
Regularized multi-defocus inversion on the CTF slice fills the zeros
of a single defocus, is unique for every positive regularization, and
is first-order consistent with bilinear FO.
Gauss--Newton iteration on the full kernel is reserved for
strong-phase objects whose linearized residual exceeds the noise;
for the one-dimensional sinusoid that exhibits CTF failure it
collapses to a Bessel fit.
These results broaden quantitative LEEM contrast analysis from
forward matching of a focal series to recovery of the object, with
the discrete linear stage verified against the same kernel used to
predict the images.

\appendix
\section{Formal claims}
\label{app:formal}

The statements below are finite-dimensional.
No existence theorem for the fast Fourier transform is claimed.

\begin{theorem}[Unique minimizer]
\label{thm:unique}
For $\alpha>0$ the energy
$J(x)=\sum_k\norm{h_k x-y_k}^2+\alpha\norm{x}^2$
has a unique global minimizer~\eqref{eq:xhat}.
Completing the square:
$J(x)=J(\hat x)+D\norm{x-\hat x}^2$ with $D=\alpha+\sum\abs{h_k}^2>0$.
\end{theorem}
Lean: \texttt{tikhonovJ\_min}, \texttt{tikhonov\_unique},
\texttt{tikhonovJ\_eq\_shift}.
The same identity gives uniqueness at $\alpha=0$ whenever
$\sum\abs{h_k}^2>0$.

\begin{theorem}[Bias--noise identity]
\label{thm:error}
If $y_k=h_k x^\star+n_k$ and $D\neq 0$, then
\begin{equation}
\hat x-x^\star
=
\frac{\sum_k\overline{h_k}\,n_k-\alpha x^\star}{D}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
Lean: \texttt{tikhonov\_error}.
Taking $\mathbb{E}[n]=0$ is informal (no probability space).

\begin{theorem}[Triangle bound, sharp]
\label{thm:bound}
If $\alpha\ge 0$ and $D>0$,
\begin{equation}
\abs{\hat x-x^\star}
\le
\frac{\sum_k\abs{h_k}\,\abs{n_k}+\alpha\,\abs{x^\star}}{D}.
\end{equation}
Equality holds for one measurement, $h=n=1$, $x^\star=-1$, $\alpha=1$.
\end{theorem}
Lean: \texttt{tikhonov\_error\_bound},
\texttt{tikhonov\_error\_bound\_sharp}.

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic remainder of bilinear FO]
\label{thm:quad}
With vacuum spectrum $1_{\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0}}$ and increment $\delta$,
\begin{equation}
\hat I(\mathrm{vac}+\delta)
=
\hat I(\mathrm{vac})
+DI_{\mathrm{vac}}[\delta]
+\hat I(\delta),
\end{equation}
where $DI_{\mathrm{vac}}[\delta](\xi)=\RFO(\xi,0)\,\delta(\xi)+\RFO(0,-\xi)\,\overline{\delta(-\xi)}$
and $\abs{\hat I(\delta)(\xi)}\le\bigl(\sum\abs{\RFO}\bigr)\bigl(\sum\abs{\delta}\bigr)^2$.
\end{theorem}
Lean: \texttt{ihat\_add}, \texttt{ihatJac\_vacuum},
\texttt{ihat\_quadratic\_remainder}, \texttt{ihat\_bound}.
The dropped term is exactly bilinear: linearized inversion is biased by
$O(\norm{\delta}^2)$, which is the whole image for a strong-phase
sinusoid~\cite{Yu2017}.

\begin{theorem}[Interior CTF zeros of a pure-defocus column]
\label{thm:ctfzero}
$\sin(2\pi\chi_S)=0$ whenever $2\chi_S\in\Z$.
For $C_3=C_5=0$, $\Dz\neq 0$, and a large enough aperture, there exists
nonzero $q$ inside the disk with that vanishing
(\texttt{exists\_interior\_weakPhase\_zero}).
A single defocus is therefore blind at those rings; $K\ge 2$ with
incommensurate $\Dz$ typically fills them.
This is a pure-defocus hypothesis, not a column that still has
spherical aberration.
\end{theorem}

The $2\times 2$ Gram determinant of the vacuum Jacobian is positive for
$\alpha>0$ (\texttt{gramDet\_pos}), so the conjugate-pair normal
equations are invertible even though one measurement is not
(\cref{sec:intro}).

\paragraph{Modelled cost.}
Set $\mathrm{dftCost}(N):=N\log_2 N$ and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{reconstructCost}(K,N)
=(K+1)\,N\log_2 N+N(8K+4).
\end{equation}
For $K\ge 1$ and $N\ge 2$,
$\mathrm{reconstructCost}(K,N)\le 14\,K\,N\log_2 N$.
At $N=128$ this is strictly cheaper than a dense bilinear apply
$KN^2$, for every $K\ge 1$
(\texttt{reconstructCost\_le},
\texttt{reconstructCost\_lt\_dense\_128}).
The constant $14$ is attained in the model at $K=1$, $N=2$.
